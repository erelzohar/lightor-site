An appointment reminder text names your business, the day and time, the address and a way to cancel or reschedule. Send a confirmation as soon as the appointment is booked and one reminder while there is still time to change plans, usually the day before or the morning of the visit. Below are 14 templates you can copy.

Most customers who miss an appointment did not plan to. They forgot, their day changed and calling felt awkward, or they mixed up the time. A short, friendly text at the right moment fixes most of those. A long one, or five of them, gets ignored.

Replace everything in [brackets] with your own details, and keep each message to one screen.

## What should an appointment reminder text include?

Five things, in this order of importance:

- **Who is texting.** Start with your business name. A number the customer does not recognize gets ignored or reported.
- **When.** The day, the date and the time. "Tomorrow" alone is risky when a text arrives after midnight.
- **Where.** The address, plus one line on parking or the entrance if people get lost.
- **How to change it.** A link or a simple reply ("Reply C to cancel") and until when it works.
- **What to bring or do**, only if it matters: arrive 10 minutes early, come without makeup, bring last year's forms.

Leave out long policies, several links and anything private. A text can be read on a lock screen by anyone nearby, so never put health details or prices of personal treatments in it.

## When should you send an appointment reminder?

Early enough that the customer can still change plans and you can still fill the slot. Each moment has its own use:

| When | What it does well | What to keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| Right after booking | Puts every detail in writing | It is a confirmation, not a reminder yet |
| The day before | Leaves time to cancel and refill the slot | Feels far away for a morning appointment |
| The morning of the day | Fresh in the customer's mind | Less time to fill a cancelled slot |
| About an hour before | Catches the person who forgot today | Leaves almost no time to change |

For most businesses, **a confirmation plus one reminder** is enough. Add a short second reminder only for long or expensive appointments. Three texts for one haircut start to feel like spam.

## Do you need the customer's permission to text them?

Yes, get it when they book. According to the [FCC's guide to unwanted robocalls and texts](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/stop-unwanted-robocalls-and-texts), automated texts to a cell phone need the person's consent, and people can opt out at any time and in any reasonable way. In practice:

- Say on the booking form that the customer will get texts about the appointment.
- Honor "STOP" and any other clear request to stop, right away.
- Keep reminders about the appointment. A promotion is a different kind of message with stricter rules.

This is general information, not legal advice. If you send marketing texts as well, read the rules that apply to them or ask a lawyer.

## Appointment reminder text templates

### Booking confirmations

**1. Standard confirmation**

> Hi [Name], you're booked at [Business] on [Day, Month Date] at [Time], [Address]. Need to change it? Use this link until [deadline]: [link]

**2. Confirmation with your cancellation rule**

> Hi [Name], your [service] at [Business] is set for [Day] at [Time]. Please give us [24 hours'] notice to cancel or move it: [link]. See you then!

**3. Confirmation for a class**

> You're in! [Class name] at [Business], [Day] at [Time]. One spot is saved for you. Can't make it? Free it up here so someone else can join: [link]

### Reminders before the appointment

**4. The day before**

> Hi [Name], a reminder from [Business]: see you tomorrow, [Day], at [Time] at [Address]. Plans changed? Reschedule or cancel here: [link]

**5. The morning of**

> Good morning [Name]! [Business] is looking forward to seeing you today at [Time]. Address: [Address]. If something came up, let us know here: [link]

**6. About an hour before**

> Hi [Name], your appointment at [Business] is in about an hour, at [Time]. See you soon!

**7. With one preparation step**

> Hi [Name], before your [service] tomorrow at [Time]: [one instruction, e.g. "please arrive with clean, dry hair"]. Questions? Just reply. To reschedule: [link]

**8. For a home visit**

> Hi [Name], [Business] here. [Technician] will arrive tomorrow between [Time] and [Time] at [Address]. Please make sure someone is home. To change the time: [link]

### When plans change

**9. Asking the customer to confirm**

> Hi [Name], are we still on for [Day] at [Time] at [Business]? Reply YES to confirm, or reschedule here: [link]

**10. You moved the appointment**

> Hi [Name], your appointment at [Business] has moved to [Day, Month Date] at [Time]. Sorry for the change. If the new time doesn't work, pick another: [link]

**11. You had to cancel**

> Hi [Name], we're sorry, but [Business] has to cancel your appointment on [Day] at [Time]. Please book a new time here: [link]

### After the appointment

**12. After a no-show**

> Hi [Name], we missed you today at [Time]. Hope everything is OK! Whenever you're ready, you can book a new time here: [link]

**13. Thank you and next visit**

> Thanks for coming in today, [Name]! When you're ready for your next [service], book here: [link]

**14. A slot just opened (for your status or stories, not a text to everyone)**

> A spot just opened at [Business]: [Day] at [Time] for [service]. First to book gets it: [link]

Keep the tone of the no-show message warm. Most people who miss once come back, unless they feel blamed.

## Text message or WhatsApp?

Let the customer choose if you can. A text reaches every phone without an app or an account. WhatsApp is the app many customers already live in, and they see the whole conversation with your business in one place. Automated WhatsApp messages, like reminders, usually go through the [WhatsApp Business Platform](https://whatsappbusiness.com/products/business-platform/), so they need a system that is connected to it.

## How to keep reminders from feeling like spam

- **One clear purpose per message.** A reminder reminds; it does not sell.
- **Same sender every time**, so the customer learns to recognize you.
- **Daytime only.** No reminder at 6 a.m. or 11 p.m.
- **Count the results.** Note how many no-shows and late cancellations you had in a month before the reminders, then a month after, and adjust the timing.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I confirm an appointment by text?

Send one short message right after the booking with your business name, the day, the time, the address and a link or reply option to change it. For example: "Hi [Name], you're booked at [Business] on [Day] at [Time]. Need to change it? [link]".

### How do I remind clients of appointments?

Pick one channel your clients read, usually a text or WhatsApp, and send a reminder the day before or the morning of the appointment. A booking system can send it automatically, so you never have to remember to remind.

### What is a no-show fee?

It is a charge some businesses set for a customer who misses an appointment without notice. If you use one, state the amount and the deadline when the customer books and in the confirmation, so it never comes as a surprise.

### How do I send appointment reminders via text?

You can type them yourself, schedule them if your phone's messaging app allows it, or use a booking system that sends them for you after each booking. Whichever you choose, get the customer's consent when they book and stop as soon as they ask.
