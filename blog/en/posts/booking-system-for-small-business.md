A booking system for a small business is an online calendar where customers see only your open times and book an appointment themselves, without calls or messages back and forth. Before you choose one, check ten things: your hours and services, what customers receive after booking, how they cancel, where the calendar lives, and what it costs as you grow.

Most small businesses start the same way: bookings by phone, by text and by direct message, written into a paper diary or a notes app. It works until it doesn't. A missed call is a missed customer, a reply sent at 11 p.m. is a working evening, and a double booking is an apology. A booking system takes that work off your hands, but only if it fits the way your business really runs.

This guide is for anyone who sells their time by the appointment or the class: salons and barbers, trainers and studios, therapists, tutors, consultants, groomers, photographers and repair shops.

## What does a booking system actually do?

It turns your schedule into a page customers can book from on their own, at any hour. A good one does four jobs at once:

- **Shows only real availability**: your working hours, minus breaks, days off and appointments already taken.
- **Collects what you need to know**: the customer's name and phone number, the service, and any question you want answered in advance.
- **Tells the customer what happens next**: a confirmation right away, a reminder before the appointment, and a way to cancel or reschedule.
- **Keeps you in the loop**: an alert when someone books or cancels, and a calendar you can manage from your phone.

Everything else is detail. The ten checks below are where those details decide whether a system helps you or gets in your way.

## 10 things to check before you choose

### 1. Does it follow your real hours?

It should know your hours for each day of the week, your breaks, a different schedule for a single date, and your vacations. If you have to block lunch by hand every single day, it will be booked over sooner or later.

### 2. Can each service have its own length and price?

A 30-minute haircut and a two-hour color are not the same slot. Open times should be worked out from the length of the service the customer picks, so a long service never squeezes into a short gap.

### 3. Does it handle classes as well as one-to-one appointments?

If you run group sessions, you need a number of places, fixed weekly times, and booking that closes when the class is full. One person per seat, and a full class should never be offered.

### 4. What does the customer get after booking?

Look for an immediate confirmation with the date, the time and a link to manage the booking, and at least one reminder before the appointment. Ask which channel it uses: a text message, WhatsApp, or email. In the United States, automated texts to a cell phone need the person's consent, and people can opt out at any time, according to the [FCC's guide to unwanted robocalls and texts](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/stop-unwanted-robocalls-and-texts). A good system asks for that consent where the customer gives the number. We put ready-to-copy wording in [appointment reminder text templates](/en/blog/appointment-reminder-text-templates/).

### 5. Can customers cancel or reschedule without calling you?

People cancel late when cancelling is awkward. A link in the confirmation that lets them move or cancel, up to a deadline you choose, gets you the free slot back while there is still time to fill it.

### 6. Can you ask your own questions in the booking form?

"Is this your first visit?", "What's the address for a house call?", "Any allergies we should know about?" The answers should arrive with the booking, not in a separate message you have to chase.

### 7. Does it live on your own website?

Some systems send customers to the platform's page, next to other businesses. A calendar that sits on your own site keeps the customer with you, and gives you one link to share everywhere: your bio, your Google profile, your messages.

### 8. Can you run it from your phone?

You will check your day between appointments, not at a desk. Make sure you can see the week, add a booking for a walk-in or a phone call, move an appointment, and cancel, all from the phone. A sync to the calendar you already use is a bonus.

### 9. What does it cost as you grow?

Free plans usually cap something: bookings a month, services, or reminders. Read what happens when you hit the cap, what the next plan costs, and whether extra bookings are billed or simply blocked. The monthly price matters less than the price at your busiest month.

### 10. Does it remember your customers?

A list of customers with their past visits, private notes and the option to block someone who keeps not showing up saves you from relying on memory.

## How do you set one up in a day?

You can be taking bookings by this evening if you follow this order:

1. **Write your services down** with a length and a price for each. Start with the five you sell most.
2. **Enter your hours**, breaks and any days off already planned.
3. **Decide your rules**: how far ahead customers can book, and until when they can cancel. Write the cancellation rule where customers will read it.
4. **Add one or two questions** to the form, not ten. Each extra field costs you a few bookings.
5. **Book a test appointment yourself** from your phone, as a customer would, and check the confirmation.
6. **Share the link**: your website, your social bio, your Google Business Profile, and the reply you send to anyone who asks "when are you free?"

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a free booking system?

Yes. Many booking systems have a free plan, usually with a cap on bookings, services or reminders each month. Lightor's free plan takes 15 bookings a month with 3 services and sends a confirmation, and its paid plans add reminders and higher limits.

### How does an online booking system work?

You enter your services, hours and rules once. The system shows customers only the times that are still open, takes their details, saves the appointment in your calendar and sends them a confirmation with a way to cancel or reschedule.

### How much does an online booking system cost?

It depends on how many bookings you take, how many people work from the same calendar, and whether reminders and payments are included. Compare the price at your busiest month, not the quietest. In Lightor, the paid plans are $29 and $49 a month.

### What is the best booking system for a small business?

The one that matches how you work: your mix of services and classes, the channel your customers read, and the way they cancel. Book a test appointment as a customer before you commit, and check what happens when you reach the free plan's limit.
