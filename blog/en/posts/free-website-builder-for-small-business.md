A free website builder for a small business lets you create and publish a site without paying, usually on the builder's own address and with its name on the page. Before you choose one, check what the free plan really includes: the address, the builder's branding, limits on pages and features, whether you can connect your own domain, and what it costs once you need more.

"Free" is a real option for a small business. A plumber who needs a page with services, an area and a phone button, or a nail artist who needs a gallery and a booking link, can run on a free plan for a long time. The trouble starts when the free plan quietly leaves out the one thing the business needed, and you find out after you built everything.

This guide walks through what free plans typically give, what they hold back, and how to pick one you will not have to leave in three months.

## What do you get with a free website builder?

Usually enough to be online today. Most free plans include:

- **An address on the builder's domain**, such as yourname.builder.com, instead of yourname.com.
- **Hosting and a security certificate**, so the site loads over https without you setting up a server.
- **Templates or an AI that writes a first draft**, which you then edit by clicking on text and pictures.
- **Mobile layouts**, because most visitors will see your site on a phone.
- **A contact form or buttons** to call, text or message you.

For many small businesses, that covers the basics customers look for: who you are, what you offer, where you are, and how to reach you.

## What do free plans usually leave out?

The things that cost the builder money or that a growing business needs most. Look for these limits in the plan's small print:

- **Your own domain.** Connecting yourname.com is often a paid feature. Even when it is included, you usually buy the domain itself from a registrar.
- **The builder's badge or ads.** Free sites often carry a "Built with" line or banner.
- **Monthly caps** on bookings, form submissions, products or AI edits.
- **Features that run your business**: online booking, reminders to customers, a blog, or payments.
- **Support.** Free plans often get help articles only.

None of these is a dealbreaker on its own. What matters is whether the cap you will hit first is one you can live with, and what the step up costs when you do.

## 7 questions to ask before you build on a free plan

1. **What will my address look like?** Say it out loud as if you were telling a customer. If it is long or hard to spell, you will want your own domain sooner.
2. **What happens when I reach a limit?** Does the site stop taking bookings, show a message, or keep working?
3. **Can I change everything myself?** Every text, picture, color and section, without a developer.
4. **Does it do what my customers need from me?** Book an appointment, ask for a quote, see products, or just call.
5. **How do search engines find it?** Look for a sitemap, the page's information marked up for search engines, and a setting to hide the site while you work on it.
6. **What does the first paid plan cost, monthly and yearly?** Plan for the month you are busiest, not the month you sign up.
7. **Can I leave?** Find out whether you can keep your domain and your content if you move.

## Can you build a free website with AI?

Yes, and it is now the fastest way to get a first version. You describe the business in plain words, and the AI writes the texts, picks a design and lays out the pages. Two things to watch:

- **Check every fact.** An AI fills gaps with reasonable guesses. Read your hours, prices, address and services line by line before you publish.
- **Make sure you can edit it afterwards** without asking the AI again for every comma.

Write a good description and you get a much better first draft. Include who you serve, the area you work in, your main services, and what you want visitors to do: book, call or send a request.

## What should a small business website have on day one?

Keep the first version short and finish it today. It needs:

- A first screen that says what you do and where, in one sentence.
- Your services, with prices or a "from" price if you can.
- A clear next step: book, call, or ask for a quote.
- Your hours and address, or your service area.
- A few real photos of your work, and reviews you were given.
- Your phone number and the messaging app your customers use.

Everything else, a blog, a long story, ten pages, can come later.

## When is it worth paying?

When the free plan starts to cost you customers. Typical moments: you want your own domain on business cards, you reach the monthly cap of bookings or requests, you want automatic reminders, or you want the builder's badge off the page. Compare that cost with one lost booking a month, and the decision usually makes itself.
